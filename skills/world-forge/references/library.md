Use these before writing your own: each part is tested with screenshots and solves a problem the first test run got wrong. Paths are relative to the project.

## 1. Materials
GPU-baked texture sets (1024 px at `high`, about 0.3 s for all of them), with one shared shader patch on every material.
- `ctx.mats.get('concrete')` gets a cached `MeshStandardMaterial`. Unknown names come back loud magenta and are logged.
- Built-in names: `concrete asphalt painted_metal painted_blue rust wood_planks bark rock snow plaster roof_tiles dirt fabric`, plus `ballast timber_red timber_cream wood_indoor sleeper tarpaper steel steel_bare paint_grey paint_black ceramic canvas ice` and the indoor set `plaster_in timber_green_in iron_in` (no snow, no wet).
- **Indoors use indoor materials** (`snow: 0, wet: [0,1,0,0]`): otherwise snow settles on desks and floors under the roof.
- `bump` sets the normal strength per material, even when materials share a recipe. **`bump: 0` = no normal map**: use it for painted plaster and paper (a scaled-down normal map still shows its steep edges as blobs).
- Keep wood grain and fine lines **out of the height (normal map)**: at grazing lamp light they turn into hard stripes. Keep pattern frequencies ≤ texture size / 8 (finer aliases into moire/plaid).
- **Add a material:** add a line to `MATERIALS` in mats.js, for example `paint_green: { recipe: 'painted_metal', metres: 2, paint: [0.17,0.37,0.27], wet: [...], snow: 1 }`. Variants share one texture set and differ by `paint` colour.
- **Add a recipe:** write `void S_name(ARGS)` in recipes.glsl.js using `fbm`, `ridged`, `voronoi`, `scratches` and `gnoise`. All of them tile when frequencies are integers. Layer the damage (base, wear, dirt, streaks, scratches) and cluster it in zones. Check the result with `?show=mats`, using close-ups through `&cam=`.
- **Weather on every surface:** `import { SURFACE } from './mats.js'`, then set `SURFACE.wet.value` and `SURFACE.snow.value` (0..1).
  - Wet surfaces get darker, more saturated and glossier, with streaks on vertical faces.
  - Snow settles on upward faces and sparkles near the camera.
  - Preview with `?show=mats&wet=1` or `&snow=1`.
- `worldUV(geo, metres)` gives box-projected UVs in metres. Call it on any geometry that uses these materials.
- **Interiors (automatic):** the first two `level.interiorBoxes` (inner wall faces) cut the sky/environment light inside to `SURFACE.indoorK` (0.22), and every pool light is tagged inside/outside so a lamp does not shine through walls (point lights have no shadows). Light a room with its own lamps: a desk lamp, a stove glow (`flicker`), a cool `lampCone` through the window.
- Merged meshes you build yourself with vertex colours: use `matVC(ctx, name)` from props.js (a copy that keeps the shared patch).

## 2. Renderer
- **Pipeline:** HDR → GTAO → bloom with an HDR threshold (only lights, the sun and flashes bloom) → final pass. The final pass does 75% ACES + 25% AgX, split-tone, lift/gain, S-curve, vignette, grain and dither. All the knobs are in `GRADE` in `art.js`.
- **Height fog:** it is patched into three's fog chunks. `SKY.fogStart` (metres) leaves clear air near the camera: in run 3's overcast town, fogStart 12 + a slightly higher density took the start view from one grey band (p5 0.15) to a dark foreground and hazy distance (p5 0.03). Its colour is the sky's horizon colour in each direction, plus a sun glow. The knobs are `SKY.fogDensity`, `fogHeight`, `fogBase` and `fogGlow`.
- **Environment light:** a PMREM made from the sky shader.
- **Shadows:** the sun's shadow is fitted to `WORLD` and refreshed every other frame. Call `ctx.render.updateShadows()` after moving anything big.
- **Light pool:** `ctx.render.lights.pulse(pos, 0xffaa66, 40, 0.08, 8)` makes a flash. `const l = ctx.render.lights.acquire()` gives a lamp or torch; release it with `release(l)`. There are eight lights in all (`LIGHT_POOL` in render.js; each costs ~0.6 ms at 1600×900 headless). Never add or remove lights at runtime: each change recompiles every shader.
- **GTAO is on only at `ultra`**: it re-renders the scene and cost ~3× frame time at 1080p headless. Prefer vertex-colour AO in props (contact darkening) and turn GTAO on for `high` only if `bench.mjs` allows.
- **Time of day:** `import { makeTOD } from '../lib/tod.js'`; `const tod = makeTOD(ctx, [{ at: 0, ...dusk }, { at: 1, ...night }]); tod.set(k)` every frame. It moves sky, fog, sun/moon (direction, colour, intensity, disc vs moon), hemisphere, environment (re-captured when it changed enough), exposure, stars and foliage rim. Keys may list only what changes. Preview: `?show=trees&snow=1&tod=1`.
- **Pixel budget:** each tier caps the internal resolution (`pixels` in quality.js: high 2.3 MP). A Retina laptop at dpr 2 would otherwise render 4× the pixels. Adaptive resolution is off in shots, clips and bench.
- `ctx.render.setExposure(v)`. `ctx.render.passes.{gtao, bloom, final}` gives direct access to the passes.
- **Sanitize pass** after the scene render: it zeroes NaN/Inf pixels and clamps at 2000. Without it, one bad pixel (a hot specular that overflows half float) is spread by the bloom blur into a big black rectangle (test run 3 lost ~5 min to it). Keep it.
- **Interior / underground environment:** `ctx.render.captureEnv(new THREE.Vector3(x, 1.7, z), { far: 120 })` after the level is built: renders the scene into a cube and uses it as the environment. Without it, glossy floors and stainless steel reflect a sky that is not there.
- **Rect area lights** (fluorescent strips, light panels): `RectAreaLightUniformsLib.init()` once, then `new THREE.RectAreaLight(color, I, w, h)` pointed with `lookAt`. Add them at load time only (like the pool) and keep them few: each one costs every lit pixel of every pass (night-metro: 18 rect lights = 194 ms headless, 6 = +10 ms). They cast no shadows: keep a weak straight-down `SUN` for contact shadows. For bay-to-bay variation (dim, dead, aged tubes) bake it: vertex colours on subdivided walls/ceilings (`seg` + a `bake(x,y,z)` function) and a darkening floor overlay, plus one flickering pool light under a failing fixture.
- **MSAA at high is 2** (4 on the half-float target doubled the frame time in night-metro: 62 → 29 ms). Use 4 only at ultra.
- **Objects that must not appear in the AO pre-pass** (shader-driven geometry, cones, particles): set `material.transparent = true` or `mesh.userData.noAO = true`.

## 3. Vegetation
```js
import { makeConifer, makeBroadleaf, makeDeadTree, foliageMaterial, barkMaterial, plantForest } from '../lib/vegetation.js';
const bark = barkMaterial(ctx), needles = foliageMaterial('needles');      // 'leaves' for broadleaf
const near = [{ tree: makeConifer({ height: 12, seed: 1 }), barkMat: bark, leafMat: needles }, ...];
const far  = [{ tree: makeConifer({ height: 12, seed: 1, detail: 0.4 }), barkMat: bark, leafMat: needles }, ...];
plantForest(ctx, near, spotsWithin25m); plantForest(ctx, far, spotsBeyond, { castShadow: false });   // spots: {x, z, y?, s?, yaw?, variant?}
// add shadowTree to near variants: the same tree at detail 0.3 casts the shadow (cut the frame ~20% in a 150-tree level)
{ tree: makeConifer({ height: 12, seed: 1 }), shadowTree: makeConifer({ height: 12, seed: 1, detail: 0.3 }), barkMat: bark, leafMat: needles }
```
- Trees are real branch tubes plus crossed cards (alpha-to-coverage). Conifer cards use a two-spray spruce **atlas** (bottle-brush shoots, no dark outline) and a **snow-pad mask**: with `SURFACE.snow` on, ragged white pads lie along the shoots of upward cards and widen with distance so far crowns read snow-laden. Crown normals point outward, crown AO follows the envelope, every spray has its own value, the moon/sun rims the crown (`FOLIAGE_RIM`, set by tod.js), sprays closer than ~1 m fade out, and alpha keeps its coverage in small mips (no bald trees at distance).
- `makeSnag({ height, seed })`: a dead spruce (straight trunk, broken top, stubs). `makeDeadTree` is a dead broadleaf.
- Instances are split into culled chunks (`chunk: 24` m); bark and crown share one transform per tree.
- **Cost:** a hero conifer is about 10–16k triangles; `detail: 0.4` is about a third of that. Each variant costs 2 draw calls, whatever the number of instances.
- Trees sit on `opts.groundHeight`, else `ctx.level.groundHeight(x, z)`. main.js gives you an empty `ctx.level` before `level.create`, so set `ctx.level.groundHeight = t.heightAt` right after making the terrain and before planting.
- Preview with `?show=trees`, `&snow=1` and `&night=1`.
- Known weakness: broadleaf crowns are clumpy up close. Conifers are the strong case.
- **Broadleaf crown shading (fixed in run 7):** `crownNormals` used a fixed 1.6 m envelope for broadleaf (no `radiusAt`), so every leaf of a 5 m crown counted as "outside" and crowns had no dark interior (flat green blobs). It now measures the real crown (radius and height range). Keep the darkening mild (inside ~0.55, underside ~0.78): stronger darkened whole slopes and lost a 3-reviewer A/B.
- **`makeBroadleaf({ leafScale, leafCount })` (run 7):** the default twig cards are ~1 m, so leaves read 20-30 cm wide at 10 m ("cartoon"). `leafScale: 0.55, leafCount: 2.6` on near trees (+~5% triangles) gave leaves at a believable size and won the A/B. For self-seeded saplings and 40-year-old floor trees use `trunk 0.1-0.25, spread 0.75-1.35, leafFrom: 4, leafy: 0.75-0.8` and mix 5-6 variants plus a `makeDeadTree`: a short trunk under a round crown reads as a lollipop. Do NOT shrink conifer sprays the same way: the fir lost its layered drooping silhouette and became a solid cone (reverted).
- **Slope trees backlit by a low sun glow** (translucency) and float as pale puffs over the darker card forest: give them their own darker leaf material.

## 4. Terrain
```js
import { makeTerrain, hills } from '../lib/terrain.js';
const t = makeTerrain(ctx, { size: 160, res: 200, height: hills(7, { amp: 2.5 }), material: 'snow',
  flatten: [{ x: 0, z: 0, r: 12, y: 0 }], paths: [{ pts: [[-30, 10], [0, 0], [20, -25]], width: 2.5 }],
  berm: { at: 45, height: 6, width: 15 } });
ctx.level.groundHeight = t.heightAt;   // set it now: trees and props planted during create read it
```
- `tint: (x, z, h, normalY) => [r, g, b]` bakes vertex colour into the terrain: tree wells, trodden paths, soot along a line, contact rings at object bases. Free detail, no textures.

## 5. Figures and creatures
```js
import { makeFigure } from '../lib/figure.js';
const f = makeFigure(ctx, { height: 2.3, thin: 1.5, armLen: 1.45, headSize: 0.8, neck: 1.6, robe: true, hairLen: 1.1, eyeGlow: 10 });
scene.add(f.root);  /* every frame: */ f.update(dt, { mode: 'stalk', speed: 0.7, lookAt: camera.position, stepRate: 9 });
```
- Eyes keep a minimum screen size (about 2 px), so a glow of 10–15 reads at 20 m.
- **Modes:** `idle` (breathing, a slow wrong head tilt), `walk`, `stalk` (hunched, arms forward) and `reach`. `stepRate` greater than 0 gives uncanny stop-motion. `setEyeGlow(k)` sets the eye glow.
- Bones are in `f.bones` (hips, spine, chest, neck, head, L/R shoulder, upper, fore, hand, thigh, shin and foot) for your own poses.
- Cloth rags and hair are world-space verlet strips that swing and lag behind.
- **People:** `dressed: true` gives sleeves, trousers and shoes (`trousers`, `shoes` colours), a cloth torso, wide sleeves; with `rags: 0`, `eyeGlow: 0`, a hat (cone on `f.bones.head`) and the face turned away or under the hat brim, daytime townspeople stop reading as bare grey mannequins. Place them where they work (a tea master behind his counter facing the room, not in front of it facing the wall).
- **Honest limit:** these read well as **creatures in darkness, fog or a glimpse**. Lit up close, people built in code look like mannequins. For a hero character the player studies up close, use an external model (§8).
- Preview with `?show=figure`; check the motion with `tools/clip.mjs`.

## 6. Weather and effects
- **Weather:** set `WEATHER` in `art.js` (`{ type: 'rain' | 'snow' | 'none', amount }`). Rain and snow are stateless GPU particles wrapped around the camera, and they switch off inside `level.interiorBoxes`.
- `ctx.fx.setLamps([{ pos, color: [1, .7, .4], range: 5 }])`: up to 4 lamps that light the falling snow and make the settled snow glint near them. fx is created before the level, so the level can call it. This is the signature shot of a snowy night.
- `ctx.fx.lampCone(pos, dir, { length, radius, color, strength })` returns an additive light-cone mesh. Add it to the scene.
- **Rain** streak quads are `DoubleSide`. Before test run 3 they were back-face culled and no rain was ever visible: if rain or any camera-facing quad shows nothing, check the side first.
- **Rainy worlds (`lib/rainy.js`, from the water-town run):** `makePuddles(ctx, spots)` glossy puddle films with rain rings; `makeHills(ctx)` two ink-wash ridge layers (flat cards: fine at eye level, bad from above); `makeWillow(ctx, { x, z, lean, height })` weeping willow with swaying strands; `makeRainStreaks(ctx, count)`; `makeEaveDrips(ctx, eaves, { spacing, groundAt })` drop lines from roof edges (`eaves` = world segments `{a, b}`).
- **Light bake (`lib/lightbake.js`), the interior lighting answer:** `makeBaker({ lights, occluders, ref, ambient, bounce, ao })` computes, once at load on the CPU, how much fixture light reaches each vertex: line or point emitters with a downward lobe + `side` spill (ceiling hot spots), occlusion by boxes and upright cylinders (columns, benches, walls), cosine-weighted AO, and `bounce` for down-facing surfaces. `bakeMesh(mesh, baker)` writes it into vertex colours (props too: skip metals, they show light by reflection). Carrier = a uniform hemisphere light (sky = ground) + the captured environment; keep 2-4 weak rect lights only for specular sheen. Results in night-metro: discrete fixtures → pools under each, scalloped pools on walls under wall-washer downlights, dark corners, the critic's "flat even light" gone. Cost: 2.6 s for ~60k vertices + 300k prop vertices with the built-in 12 cm cache (105 s without it). Dim a section later by scaling the stored `userData.baked` colours (no re-bake).
- **Volumetric fog (`lib/volfog.js`, run 6):** set `FOG_VOL = { on: true, density, base, height, banks, bankScale, g, sun, amb, maxDist, steps, inDust }` in art.js and render.js adds a post pass that marches each pixel's ray to the scene depth, lit by the SUN'S SHADOW MAP. One effect gives valley fog banks that glow toward a low sun AND the light shafts through broken roofs, windows and tree crowns (the shafts are lit fog between shadows). `ctx.render.passes.volfog.setInterior([[min,max], ...])` makes interiors still dusty air (no banks). Keep the fog LOW (height ~7 m, patchy banks) so slopes and ridges stand above it; a thick uniform layer reads as a beige wash. Cost ~13 ms at 30 steps headless. Bug fixed in render.js: without MSAA the composer's two buffers shared one depth texture (a feedback loop -> a BLACK frame on low/test tiers): each buffer now has its own.
- **Big worlds:** `export const SHADOW_FOLLOW = 110` (metres) in art.js: the sun's shadow box follows the camera. `SKY.clouds` (0..1): a broken cloud deck in the sky shader, lit by the sun (warm undersides near it, silver edges). Material fog `SKY.fogDensity` ~0.0026 with `fogHeight` ~120 gives aerial perspective (far slopes soften); without it card forests read as cut paper.
- **Forests at valley scale (`lib/forestcards.js`):** tens of thousands of conifer cards (8 canvas shapes, ~4 tris each) for slopes beyond ~150 m; real 3D conifers (`makeConifer`, detail 0.22, no shadows) in the band nearer than that, real hero trees close. Shade each card by the slope's aspect to the sun and darken gullies (pass `k`), vary heights 0.6-1.3x; critics called uniform cards "stamped cutouts" four times. Keep slopes ~25-35 deg: near-vertical forested slopes read as "walls".
- **PHOTO VEGETATION ATLASES (run 6, the biggest single gain of the night):** code-drawn leaves, needles and tree silhouettes were called 'cel-shaded / toon / bonsai / lollipop' in 8 calibrations in a row, whatever the geometry. Generate them as photos instead (n4d nano-banana-pro, 12 credits each, `references/external-models.md`): 'botanical scan on pure flat white, a 3x3 grid of nine leafy twigs' (leaves), 'two fir sprays side by side, vertical' (needles, 9:16), 'eight whole trees in a 4x2 grid' (billboards). Key the white out in Python (alpha from brightness and saturation, erode 1 px, fill every transparent pixel with the NEAREST solid colour via scipy distance_transform_edt, or mips pull white and distant trees go white), segment whole trees with scipy.ndimage.label (crop a ruled grid by cells). Use: `foliageMaterial('leaves', { atlas })` + `makeBroadleaf({ cells: 3 })`; `foliageMaterial('needles', { atlas })` (512x1024, two sprays like the drawn atlas); `makeForestCards(ctx, spots, { atlas, broadHalf: true })`; `makeGrass(ctx, spots, { kind: 'bush', atlas })`. Darken needle colour (~0.5): a photo atlas is much brighter than the drawn one.
- **Lit billboard forest (forestcards.js v2):** ONE camera-facing quad per tree (turns about its vertical axis), 16 shapes: 8 conifers + 8 broadleaf crowns (`broad: true`), per-instance `tint` (autumn yellows/rusts). The fragment shader gives each card a cone (conifer) or dome (broadleaf) normal, so the sun lights one side of every crown: a slope reads as thousands of lit volumes instead of a painted backdrop. Sample a near band of REAL 3D trees at random from the whole band (filling it in loop order made a visible slab of rows).
- **Broadleaf for secondary growth:** `makeBroadleaf({ trunk: 0.2, leafFrom: 3, spread: 1.05, lite: true })`: a low fork, leaves on more levels, scattered loosely (packed tip pads read as 'bonsai' / 'lollipop'), and lite bark (no twig tubes; bark was 5.7 M of 14 M triangles before). Forty years of abandonment = trees on the factory floor between buildings, not only on the slopes.
- **Grass (`lib/grass.js`):** blades now mix species per tuft: seeding stalks with a thin plume, broad weeds, rust-red dead blades (one green-yellow hue was called 'stylized' twice). Plume size is in tuft units: keep it ~1 cm wide, it is scaled up with the tuft. `kind: 'blades'` makes real curved blade geometry per tuft (vertex-coloured, wind, backlit translucency), chunked per 50 m and hidden beyond 55 m. Crossed alpha cards were called "blocky alpha cards" twice. `kind: 'bush'`: ~26 leaf cards in an ellipsoid (4 crossed cards read as "flat spheres").
- **Gravity grime (mats.js `age: 0..1`):** dark vertical runs on walls, a sheet of grime and a damp/moss foot band, all from world position, so decay follows gravity instead of random blotches; with photo scans set `contrast: 0.6` in photo.js SETS to calm busy blotches. `ground: 1` on the terrain material: straw / bare earth / moss patches at 3-20 m.
- **Rails:** sweep a flat-bottom rail section (foot, web, head) along the curve, not a tube ('rails bend like rubber tubes'); scatter ballast stones; keep the curve radius real (≥ 100 m on a spur).
- **Block floors:** a walkable ground floor needs the plinth top AT floor level; a plinth 0.15 m proud buried every paper, puddle and debris piece on the floor for hours, unseen in wide shots.
- **Texture bombing (mats.js `bomb: 1`, `bombRot: 0` for bricks):** a second shifted/turned sample blended in by world noise hides the repeat of a photo scan on long walls ("the same rust-patch decal tiled" was a critic constant).
- **Mirrors and perf:** hide all InstancedMesh vegetation and the terrain from planar mirrors (`hideInMirror`). Gloss floors take `every: 2` (update every 2nd frame). Water skips its rain-ring maths when `rain` is 0. PERF TRAP: a stub that pushes `undefined` into another mirror's hideInMirror makes that mirror throw in onBeforeRender, the frame aborts, and the bench shows a fake 6x speed-up; guard every cross-push.
- **Night rain (from run 5, the rain-fuel station):** rain is seen only where light catches it. `ctx.fx.setRainLamps([{ pos, color: [HDR], range }])` (up to 6) lights the streaks, with a forward flare when the lamp is behind a drop; `ctx.fx.setRainLamp(i, pos, color, range)` moves one (a car's headlights); `ctx.fx.setRoofs([[x0, z0, x1, z1, y]])` keeps rain and splashes out from under canopies and eaves. There are two layers: near (0-13 m) and `rainFar` (9-48 m, lamp-lit only: the curtain in front of a lit canopy). `WEATHER.rainAmb` sets how bright unlit rain is (night ~0.18). Each drop has its own width, length and brightness: in a blind A/B, uniform streaks read as "a thin overlay, scratches". `ctx.fx.splash` makes stateless splash crowns on the ground, lit by the same lamps. Hide `fx.rain`, `fx.rainFar` and `fx.splash` from every mirror.
- **Haze (`lib/haze.js`):** `makeHaze(ctx, [{ min, max, radius, color }], { strength: 0.03, exclude: [min, max] })`. Lit rain mist around lights, drawn as an additive back-face box with 10 samples and a forward-scattering phase. `exclude` keeps it out of an interior. In a blind A/B, the hazier version beat the "crisper air" one (lights bloom into the mist, rain reads as volume). Keep it.
- **Fog-free interior:** `import { FOG_CLEAR } from './render.js'; FOG_CLEAR[0].set(...min); FOG_CLEAR[1].set(...max)`. The part of each view ray inside the box gets no height fog, so a shop seen through its glass, or walked into, is not foggy.
- **Grass and weeds (`lib/grass.js`):** `makeGrass(ctx, [{ x, y, z, s, dry, light }])` puts tufts on crossed cards, instanced, with wind sway. Plant them where paving meets the ground (slab edges, verges, ditches, weeds in joints). In a baked scene, pass `light` per tuft from `baker.at`.
- **Baked scene + trees:** `plantForest` spots take `light: [r, g, b]` (the bake at the crown, facing the light). In a scene lit by a bake with a uniform carrier, any unbaked mesh glows at full carrier light, so bake or tint everything. Moving props: restore their unlit colours and re-bake (`level.relight(g)` in rain-fuel). Light-bake emitters take `lobe` (> 1 gives a tighter downlight with distinct pools). Conifers closer than ~20 m to the camera at night read as sparse scribbles: keep the forest edge back.
- **Gloss/wet ground:** the blur is mip-filtered (the mirror target has mipmaps; 9 taps along screen-vertical at a coarser level). Fixed wide taps printed ghost copies of each light (rings); jittered taps fixed that but read as "sparkly noise"; mips are smooth and give real wet-asphalt streaks. Rain rings bend the reflection in standing water (blue mask). Keep the ring amplitude small: critics read strong ripples as "sparkly noise".
- **Recipes:** `smooth` is factory or emulsion paint with no pits. It has faint vertical rain streaks, speckle and scuffs, and you colour it with `paint` (`plaster` has peel spots that read as holes on white walls). `strips` is a linear aluminium strip ceiling (canopies, walkways). `lib/photo.js` is now a stub wired into mats (`photo: '<set>'`); copy `photo.example.js` over it to use CC0 scans. Its `plain(..., { contrast, sat })` calms a blotchy scan; a raw concrete scan read as "green marble".
- **Photo materials (`lib/photo.example.js`), only when the user allows external assets:** CC0 scans (ambientCG, Poly Haven; fetch with a normal browser user agent, the API at ambientcg.com/api/v2/full_json?q=...&include=previewData) for the surfaces the camera sees most. Code keeps the layout: the night-metro floor is a granite photo composed at load into 0.6 m tiles (per-tile crop and tint, grout, polished roughness remap), so joints stay aligned with world 0 and the gloss mask. In MATERIALS add `photo: '<set>'` (the recipe stays as the fallback); paint tints still apply. Five sets at 512-1024 px = 1.4 MB inlined. Keep a LICENSE note beside the files. In run 4 this was the step after the code-only plateau (~6.5 on the calibrated scale); check the A/B before keeping it.
- **Architecture (`lib/arch.js`):** `archKit(ctx, { bake })` builds walls, floors, ceilings and stairs as merged per-material meshes with tile-aligned UVs (no per-prop offset) and optional baked light in vertex colours (`seg` subdivides, `lit: true` applies `bake(x, y, z)`). Use it for any interior or station; props keep using `kit()`.
- **Gloss floor (`lib/gloss.js`):** `makeGlossFloor(ctx, { x0, x1, z0, z1, y: 0.002, strength: 1.3, blur: 1.6, tile: 0.6 })` lays an additive planar mirror over a polished floor (granite, terrazzo, a lacquered stage) that keeps its own PBR material. Fresnel, a tile-joint mask (no reflection in the grout), scuffed zones blur and dim, a slow wobble (more turns light strips into zigzags). `gloss.setGrime(tex, [x0, z0, w, d])`: the texture's RED channel removes the reflection (dirt, gum). One extra scene render (scale 0.4 at high).
- **Water (`lib/water.js`):** `makeWater(ctx, { x0, x1, z0, z1, y })` (alias `makeCanal`) is a planar mirror (three's `Reflector`) read by a custom shader: rain rings, drift ripples, Fresnel between a dark jade body colour and the reflection, darker water against the quay walls, height fog. `street: true` (with `scale: 0.3, msaa: false`) makes puddles on paving; use ONE street mirror over both banks with `hole: [z0, z1]` cutting out the canal (two mirrors cost a full extra scene render: 63 → 55 ms): noise-masked standing water with a soaked halo. Put camera-wrapped effects (rain) in `water.hideInMirror`, call `water.update(dt)` every frame. **Gotcha:** the mirror plane is the mesh's local XY. Rotate the MESH (`rotation.x = -π/2`), never the geometry, or it silently mirrors a vertical plane and still looks plausibly blurry (run 3 found this after 9 iterations). Cost: each mirror re-renders the scene (canal + street mirror = 3 renders at high). Use `scale` ≤ 0.5 and drop the street mirror at low.
- `ctx.fx.burst(pos, { count, speed, life, size, color: [HDR], gravity, drag })` emits a CPU particle pool in one draw call. For layered effects, see fx-weather.md.

## 7. Audio
- `RECIPES(ac, out, K)` in audio.js holds the synthesised sounds: `click`, `thud`, `step`, `heartbeat`, `metal`, `pickup`, `clunk`, `relay`, `creak`, `sting`, `bell` (bronze hand bell), `deliver` (a letter on a counter + two knocks), `drip` (one drop into a puddle) and `horn`. `K.gain()` starts at 0: a source that starts before its `K.env` stays silent instead of leaking at full volume.
- `setIntensity(0..1)` swells a dread drone; inside `level.interiorBoxes` the weather bed is muffled. Play one with `ctx.audio.play('step', { surface: 'snow' })`.
- **Footsteps** (the user's first complaint in the test demo was "the footsteps don't sound real"; two causes, both fixed in the template):
  - *Cadence.* One step per bob cycle, and the cycle length is a stride: `TUNE.stride + TUNE.strideK * speed` (1.3 m + 0.04·v). Walk 4.2 m/s gives 2.9 steps/s, sprint 4.3, crouch 1.6. The old `bobFreq` gave 8 steps/s: a machine gun. If you change the walk speed, check the cadence stays between 2.5 and 3.2 steps/s.
  - *Sound.* `step` plays one of 8 pre-rendered variants per surface: `snow`, `ballast` (stones between sleepers), `sleeper`, `rail`, `wood` (outdoor planks), `floor` (indoor boards), `wet` (wet flagstones: slap + squeezed water film), `stone` (dry stone stairs), `hard` (concrete, asphalt; also the fallback). Each is built per sample from layers, each scaled to a target peak: **the boot impact is the loudest layer** (noise through a low-pass falling from ~1.5 kHz to ~250 Hz in 50 ms); then the surface texture riding the load: snow = dense, slightly pitched crunch grains (band-pass 0.5–3 kHz) with a soft 15–30 ms attack and a cold-snow squeak (stick-slip pulse train) on half the steps; ballast = sparse high-Q stone clacks that keep settling; sleeper = a dull short "tok"; rail = damped inharmonic steel partials; planks = short low modes. **One impact per step**, then one continuous texture while the weight rolls heel → toe (~0.25 s, so at walking pace each step nearly reaches the next). A separate second toe hit 0.07–0.12 s later made "da-dum da-dum"; the user heard it as "像在骑马" (a galloping horse). Low-pass 4–9 kHz and high-pass 50–120 Hz per surface (a step has a thump, not a rumble). Second user verdict on a crackle-first version: "还没有特别像脚步", so keep the impact first.
  - On a railway, pick the surface from the foot position: rail head (|z| near the gauge line), sleeper (x within 0.125 m of a sleeper centre), else ballast. The demo's first version played one "gravel with pebble pings" sound on the whole track and the user heard it as wrong.
  - *Timing.* `player:step` carries `ago` (how long before this frame the foot landed). Audio schedules each step at `currentTime + 0.05 − ago`, so the rhythm is even at any frame rate (before: jitter of one frame, cv 0.05–0.1 at 30 fps).
  - *Space.* Steps go to their own bus: dry outdoors (open air and snow have almost no echo; the big sfx reverb made them sound like a hall), a short 0.4 s room tail inside `interiorBoxes`. `player:land` plays a heavy step.
  - Return the surface from `level.surfaceAt(x, z)`: interiors `floor`, platforms `wood`, ballast `hard`, the rest `snow` (or your world's equivalents). To add a surface, add an entry in `synthStep`'s table `S` and a level in `STEP_LEVEL`. Synthesis cannot replace your ears: if the user still says steps sound wrong, ask to use a few CC0 recordings (e.g. Kenney's packs) as a reference or directly.
- `SPACE` in audio.js sets the acoustic space: `bed: 'wind' | 'station' | 'none'` (station = fluorescent hum at 100/200/300 Hz, ventilation roar, tunnel air in slow gusts) and the step reverb: `stepRoom` seconds and `stepSend` [outside, inside]. A tiled underground hall: `{ bed: 'station', stepRoom: 1.7, stepSend: [0.32, 0.32] }`. More recipes: `chime` (two-tone 叮咚), `flicker` (starter clack + buzz), `lock`, `switch`, `pen`, `train` (a pass-by in a tunnel).
- There is an ambience bed with three noise layers plus slow gusts (`bed: 'wind'`). For rain set `WEATHER_SOUND = { rain: 1, drips: 1 }` in audio.js: a looped patter of ~2400 tiny drop impacts (this is what makes the bed read as rain, not wind) and random eave drips.
- `node tools/audiocheck.mjs` renders every recipe (and each step surface) offline and reports length, peak, RMS and clipping. Run it after adding sounds.
- `node tools/listen.mjs out.wav walk` (or a JSON sequence `'[[0,"horn"],[1.5,"step",{"surface":"snow"}]]'`) renders a WAV plus `out.png` (waveform + log-frequency spectrogram). Read the PNG: you can *see* full-band hiss (energy flat to 16 kHz), drum-like boom (low modes ringing > 0.1 s), buzz (harmonic stripes), a missing second contact. Then give the user the WAV path (`afplay out.wav`) so they can listen without starting the game.

## 8b. Props
- Write your prop builders in `src/modules/props_world.js` (main.js imports it). Importing them from props.js would be circular and leave `register` undefined at load.
- In each builder: `SIZES.name = [w, h, d, material, surface]`, then `register('name', (ctx, o) => { const k = kit(ctx, o.seed); k.add(bevelBox(...), 'material', { pos, rot, color, dirt }); ... return k.finish(); })`.
- Soft things are not boxes: sacks, coats and cushions are lathe or sphere shapes with creases (see a sack: profile + `sin(angle*9)` creases + slump).
- **Shoot every hero prop from three sides, one of them end-on** (`?cam=` 1.5 m away). The sheet views are too far to show a part on the wrong side. In the test demo the user found a bench whose backrest slats floated behind the frame: an `ExtrudeGeometry` profile turned with `rotateY(+π/2)` maps the profile's −x to **+z**, so the frame was mirrored. After a `rotateY(±π/2)`, check which way the profile faces, then place the parts from the profile's own coordinates (e.g. back slats on the post line `z = z0 − slope·(y − y0)`), not by eye.
- Fine repeating patterns (weave, knurl, mesh) in a normal map show as stripes and zigzags where box-projected UVs stretch on curved shapes. For cloth at prop scale use `bump: 0` and keep the weave out of the albedo contrast; the shape's creases carry the look. Real thread size is 1–2 mm (`metres` ≈ 0.12 for 64 cells).

## 10. Recipes from run 7 (Factory 712; code in `world-forge-test/sanxian`, not yet in the template)
Copy and adapt these from that project when the world needs them; each one won a blind A/B or fixed a named flaw.
- **Bounce boxes (fake GI)** — `mats.js` `SURFACE.bMin/bMax/bCol` (4 boxes) and one line in the `lights_fragment_end` patch: `irradiance += wfBounce(vWPos) * (1.0 - 0.7 * clamp(vWNrm.y, 0.0, 1.0));`. Without GI a half-roofless hall had black columns while ref photos show them bright; bounce 1.6 (warm grey) in the hall, 0.15 in a window-lit office (let the light fall off from the windows instead), 0.7 under dormitory walkways (walls there crushed to black). Less on up-facing surfaces, or floors wash out and puddles lose contrast. Independent of `indoorK` (raising indoorK changed almost nothing: the darkness was missing bounce, not sky).
- **Paving grime layer** — material flag `pave: 0..1` (`uPave`): damp blotches, moss in low spots, rust/oil stains and grit at 0.5-5 m scales on up-facing surfaces. Roads, aprons, factory floors stopped reading as "smooth textureless grey". Pair with a dry interior floor material (`wet: [0,1,0,0]`) so only the gloss-layer puddles mirror (a uniformly wet floor reads as one dark sheet).
- **Weeds follow the paving** — keep grass off paved rects except a thin edge band, the joints (per-joint random density: `jd = r()*r()`, some joints bare, a few lush) and the cracks. Uniform scatter on concrete reads as a lawn; dense bands at every joint read as hedges.
- **Grass in stands** — after placing tufts, set `dry` and size from low-frequency noise (straw patches, greener hollows, tall/short stands), not per tuft (salt-and-pepper). Keep the mean height: a version that lowered it lost an A/B ("grass got shorter").
- **`block({ breach: { end, f0 }, tint })`** — a gable corner come down from floor `f0`: jagged stepped brick stubs, hanging slab with rebar, the roof bay above fallen, no floating gable triangle, a rubble heap at the foot. `tint` gives each building its own weathering colour.
- **Old glass** — milky, grimy, translucent panes (opacity ~0.42, roughness ~0.38, no emissive: emissive made shards look like beige paper) with ~35% broken (corner shards), ~25% missing; rough enough that a 7° sun does not glare white in the start view.
- **Decals that read as age** — leak `stain` (ochre-brown runs that blotch and seep sideways, a ragged damp band at the slab line; uniform orange runs read as "painted stripes"), `mould` (black-green colonies), `silt` (dried puddle film: small blobs inside the square, soft edge; big blobs clipped by a round mask read as polka dots), seeded.
- **Paper on floors** — A4 / half / notes, yellowed by different amounts, folded and lifting at one edge (`F.paper(rr)`); uniform white quads read as decals.
- **Room "left in a hurry"** — a ceiling patch down (dark hole, lath strips, plaster heap + damp under it), a bookcase fallen on its face with spilled binders, a crate, bottles, contact `blob`s under every piece of furniture. This was the first interior change a reviewer described as "something happened to the room".
- **`tools/pick.mjs "view=x" '[[u,v],...]'`** — what is under a screen point (object, material, instance count, hit position). Use it before guessing.

### Recipes from run 8 (pine halt extension, night-metro street)
- **Stairs:** a player wider than a tread (radius 0.32 m vs 0.2 m treads) touches 2-3 steps at once; their combined rise exceeds the 0.35 m step-up and the climb succeeds only when the frame order is lucky. Flag tread colliders `stair: true` and treat them as floor only (never a horizontal wall), floor reach 0.6 m. Then any rise works at any frame rate.
- **Walk tests:** never 'hold W for N ms'. Under load the sim runs fewer, longer frames and the walk ends early (a correct stair looked broken three times). Hold W until a position test passes, with a timeout.
- **Clearing trees for a new set piece:** splice the spot list AFTER it is generated. Adding the area to the rejection test shifts every later rng draw (variants, sizes) and reshuffles the whole forest, which then loses or wins A/Bs for the wrong reason.
- **Chimney smoke at night:** additive particle systems glow; use a dozen normal-blend billboards per chimney (rise, grow, drift, fade) instead.
- **Facade age at street scale:** a painted grime canvas per facade built from the same window/AC list (sill streaks 0.1-0.25 m wide at alpha 0.35-0.65, AC rust trails, parapet wash, splash band) read in a blind A/B; thin 1-3 px streaks at alpha 0.1-0.3 were invisible.
- **Lighting A/B before believing a critic:** one critic asked to lift mid-tones and add lamp cones, the blind A/B of exactly that lost 5 vs 6 ('a yellow-grey wash'). Test conflicting advice, do not apply it.

### Promo reels (tools/reel.mjs, tools/ambience.mjs, tools/promo.mjs)
A showcase video of the world, rendered offline so even a 3 M-triangle scene plays smooth at 1080p30.
- `src/reel_shots.js` exports `SHOTS` ({ dur, q: 'url params', keys: [[camPos], [lookAt]], ... }) and `ORDER`. Hook in main.js: with `?reel=<name>` build two `CatmullRomCurve3` (positions, look points) and, in tick before the pinned-camera lines, set `pinned` from `getPoint(u)`, u = an eased reelT / dur. Add params that keep people out (`notall`, `nofig`) and make set pieces move by time (`trainv`).
- `node tools/reel.mjs <dir> [shots] --size 1920x1080 --fps 30`: `?clip=1&shot=1&reel=..`, the overlays hidden with `body > div:not(#app)` (NOT `body > div`: the canvas lives in #app), JPEG frames, H.264 per shot, frames deleted.
- Preview every shot at 640x360 / 15 fps first and read a 5-frame strip per shot: in both projects 3-4 of 12-16 first framings were inside a wall, behind a tree, too close or through a lamp head.
